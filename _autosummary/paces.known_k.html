paces.known_k#

Known-K change points: the step count a human gave turns peak-picking into exact change-point detection (paces#39).

The ask page asks for the number of steps first (docs/alignment/07 §7.3 step 1): one integer turns “find the peaks of a novelty curve” into “find the K-1 change points that best split this signal”, which kernel change-point detection solves exactly (docs/alignment/05 §7, 07 §2.16). The known-k capability is that consumer of k:

  • features: a frame-wise description of the media’s audio (audio_features(): MFCCs and their spectral contrast, each dimension standardised), through paces’ own audio decode (the [audio] extra);

  • cuts: known_k_cuts(), ruptures.KernelCPD(...).predict(n_bkps=K-1) over those frames, no cut closer than min_step_s to another or to an end (the [cpd] extra; ruptures is BSD-2-Clause);

  • names: zipped on when a step list of exactly K was given, else honestly unnamed (the explicit segmenter’s rule, paces.segmenters.steps_on_boundaries()).

The boundaries are a proposal: the confidence is low (DFLT_KNOWN_K_CONFIDENCE) and the result is flagged boundaries-proposed for the ask page to confirm. A K the media is too short to hold (fewer than min_step_s per step) is refused, with a reason, never a made-up split.

The pure core, known_k_cuts(), runs on any feature matrix:

>>> import numpy as np
>>> x = np.repeat([[0.0], [5.0], [0.0]], 50, axis=0)  # three flat steps (needs ruptures)
>>> known_k_cuts(x, 3, frame_s=0.1, min_step_s=1.0)
[5.0, 10.0]
